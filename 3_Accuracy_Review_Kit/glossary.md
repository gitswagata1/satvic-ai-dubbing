# Satvic Movement Dubbing Glossary (Hindi → Telugu / Malayalam / Kannada)

Purpose: locked terminology decisions made BEFORE translation, so every dubbed video
uses the same words. Native reviewers verify against this sheet, not their personal preference.

## Rule classes
1. **Keep as-is (brand / Sanskrit-origin)** — transliterate into the target script, never translate:
   - Satvic / Satvic Movement (brand name)
   - Names: Subah, Subah Saraf
   - Ayurvedic terms used as terms of art: prakriti, agni, ama, dosha (if spoken)
2. **Keep English loanwords natives actually use** — do not force-translate:
   - fibre, juice, smoothie, enema, toilet, motion (in the bowel sense), tension, stress
   - Numbers with units: "15 minute", "1 glass", "500 ml" — render in target-language words
     (TTS garbles bare digits in Indic scripts; spell out numbers)
3. **Translate to the natural regional word** (native reviewer confirms):
   - constipation → Telugu: మలబద్ధకం (malabaddhakam) / Malayalam: മലബന്ധം (malabandham) / Kannada: ಮಲಬದ್ಧತೆ (malabaddhate)
   - bowel movement / मल त्याग → idiomatic equivalent, NOT literal word-by-word
   - खान-पान / आहार → diet words natural to each language
4. **Colloquial, not pure** (founder feedback, locked): Subah's Hindi is everyday Hinglish,
   not शुद्ध हिन्दी — the dubs must match. Use the words people say at home, not the words
   a textbook prefers. When natives would say the English word (video, position, research,
   tool, energy, signal, permanent, solution), KEEP it in English transliteration rather
   than reaching for a Sanskritized equivalent. Literary/pure vocabulary is a defect:
   e.g. Telugu "తొలగించే" → prefer everyday phrasing like "పోగొట్టే"; avoid bookish
   nouns where a simple verb phrase works. Test: would a Telugu/Malayalam YouTuber
   talking to camera say this sentence? If not, rewrite.
5. **Register**: Subah addresses viewers respectfully but warmly (आप-form).
   - Telugu: మీరు-form throughout
   - Malayalam: നിങ്ങൾ-form
   - Kannada: ನೀವು-form
   - Health guidance must sound like a caring teacher, not a machine or a doctor's leaflet.
5. **Health-claim lines** get back-translation QC (see review sheet) — any sentence with
   a dosage, duration, "cures/heals" claim, or a do/don't instruction.
